A Survey of Lightweight Pre-trained Models for Text Classification

Abstract

Text classification is a fundamental task in natural language processing.
Traditional methods rely on hand-crafted features and shallow models.
Recent advances in pre-trained language models have changed this landscape.
In this survey, we review lightweight variants suitable for edge devices.

Keywords: text classification, pre-trained models, model compression

1 Introduction

Deep learning has dramatically improved text classification performance.
However, large models are expensive to deploy on resource-constrained devices.
Lightweight models offer a promising trade-off between accuracy and speed.
We summarize existing approaches along three dimensions.

1.1 Motivation

Edge devices such as phones and IoT sensors have strict memory budgets.
A model with billions of parameters cannot fit into these environments.

1.2 Contributions

Our contributions are three-fold.
First, we unify the taxonomy of compression techniques.
Second, we benchmark eight models on four public datasets.
Third, we release a reproducible evaluation toolkit.

2 Related Work

2.1 Knowledge Distillation

Hinton et al. proposed the teacher-student framework.
The student mimics the soft labels produced by the teacher.

2.2 Quantization

Quantization reduces the numerical precision of weights.
Eight-bit quantization typically halves the model size with minor loss.

4 Conclusion

Lightweight pre-trained models achieve competitive accuracy with far fewer resources.
We hope this survey guides practitioners in selecting appropriate models.
